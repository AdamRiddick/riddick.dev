# Repeatable article workflow

Use this for a new article or a substantial revision. Scale the work to the request; a small edit should remain small.

## Establish the brief

Use the conversation or [the article brief](../assets/article-brief.md) to identify the reader, useful question, central judgement and supplied experience. Reuse material already available rather than asking Adam to repeat it.

Separate the supporting material into:

- Adam's supplied facts and experience.
- Primary technical sources to verify or cite.
- Clearly illustrative examples.
- Interpretations and editorial positions.
- Gaps that affect a factual claim.

The CV supports broad experience and the approved outcomes; it does not prove an exact incident, date, rejected alternative, team reaction or implementation detail. Maintain the masking contract from the site's brand guide.

When the available facts support a useful general analysis, write that without pretending it is a case study. Ask a focused question only when a missing detail materially changes the argument. Put unresolved editorial questions outside the public article body.

## Shape and draft

Read the current voice profile, the site's brand guide and any designated reference article relevant to the task. If none is designated, use the working profile and name that limitation when it matters. Do not repeatedly request examples Adam has said are unavailable.

Outline the argument around the real decision: constraints, alternatives, accepted costs, consequences and conditions for choosing differently. Use this to test the reasoning rather than to force a form onto the page.

Draft in ordinary prose. Put the central point near the opening. Use an original diagram, comparison or concrete example where it helps. Keep figures and visuals within the site's theme.

For the main essays, roughly 1,000–1,600 words is a starting range, not a quota. Give a small subject a shorter treatment and a complex subject enough space. Do not pad a draft to look substantial.

A personal judgement can be written in first person; an invented personal history cannot. Keep contributions specific and team delivery visible.

## Review separately for substance and voice

Substance:

- Can each first-person factual claim be traced to supplied material?
- Are primary sources linked where the reader needs them, and do they support the claim made?
- Are alternatives represented fairly and limits made explicit?
- Are results distinguished from intent, interpretation and illustration?
- Are sensitive details and masked figures protected?
- Is the actual publication month distinct from the period discussed?

Voice:

- Does this sound like a person explaining an important decision to a colleague?
- Is the argument clear without grandstanding, heroic framing or needless jargon?
- Are the paragraphs connected, with lists and diagrams doing useful work?
- Does "I" identify Adam's part accurately and "we" describe supported collective work?
- Have filler, stock transitions and forced punchlines been removed without flattening personality?
- Does the ending leave the reader with a practical implication or a useful limit?

Do not use a numeric voice score, a word blacklist or a text-pattern test as proof that the prose is Adam's. Editorial judgement and his revisions are the calibration.

## Use Adam's edits to improve the next draft

Interpret feedback before changing the reusable profile:

| Feedback type                               | Action                                                                                               |
| ------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Factual correction                          | Fix the article and its factual notes; change shared facts only if the correction applies there too. |
| Clear general voice preference              | Update the relevant guidance and dated revision record in `voice.md`.                                |
| A single sentence replacement               | Apply it locally; look for an explicit general preference before changing the voice guide.           |
| Layout or diagram request                   | Update the relevant design guidance when general; do not misclassify it as a prose habit.            |
| Topic-specific reasoning change             | Revise the argument; preserve the more general profile.                                              |
| Article explicitly chosen as representative | Add its repository path or supplied link as a voice reference.                                       |

Apply new feedback without discarding earlier compatible preferences. Treat silence and an unedited generated draft as neutral, not as approval of its voice. Do not recast every edit as a permanent rule.

When feedback is genuinely ambiguous, make the requested local change and ask only the small question needed to decide whether it should guide future articles.

## Handoff

Deliver the requested draft or edit. If gaps remain, state them briefly outside the article. Keep source notes and agent working material out of the rendered article.

Saving or updating this style system does not publish an article. Any repository push, site publication or message follows the user's actual authorisation in the active task; do not introduce a separate approval requirement merely because this workflow exists.
