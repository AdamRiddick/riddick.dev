# Adam Riddick writing

Produce agent-assisted writing that reflects Adam's current preferences and supplied experience. The voice is a working profile, not a claim of a fully calibrated imitation. Adam currently has no representative long-form samples at hand and wants the profile to develop through article edits.

Read [the voice profile](references/voice.md) before drafting or revising. For a new article or substantial rewrite, also read [the article workflow](references/workflow.md). Use [the brief](assets/article-brief.md) when gathering a topic's facts; do not make Adam fill it out if the conversation already supplies the information.

## Essential behaviour

- The latest request and Adam's explicit edits take precedence over the profile.
- Use an approachable, technically credible voice, ordinary British English and connected prose. Keep team contribution and Adam's responsibility distinct.
- Explain the situation, choices and consequences without turning every article into an ADR form. Open with the reader's actual problem.
- Use first person for supported responsibility or an explicitly framed opinion. Never invent a past project, incident, conversation, team reaction or measured outcome to make the voice feel personal.
- Separate facts from style evidence. The CV establishes supplied facts; an agent draft does not establish Adam's natural prose. Reference publications inform the editorial approach, not his identity or phrasing.
- Keep publication month/year distinct from the period discussed. Preserve the site's agreed masking and theme when adapting content or producing diagrams.
- Browse primary sources when checking technical claims or current facts. If experience or evidence is missing, draft a general analysis or leave a clear editorial question outside the public article body.
- Keep private notes and any raw sensitive source material out of public files. A style guide does not authorise publishing drafts, sending messages or changing hosting.

## Learn from revisions

After Adam edits a piece, distinguish voice changes from factual corrections, topic-specific changes and layout requests. Apply a clearly general preference to the relevant part of `references/voice.md`; keep situational edits local. Record the reason and date briefly. Do not turn every replacement word into a permanent ban or imitate typos.

Only designate an article as a voice reference when Adam identifies it as representative. Add its repository path or supplied link to the profile, without copying private samples. An unedited draft, silence or publication by itself is not evidence that every stylistic choice matches his prose.

Keep the entrypoint lean and detailed guidance in the linked resources. Preserve a single canonical skill in the repository; the personal skill location may point to it so both copies do not drift.
